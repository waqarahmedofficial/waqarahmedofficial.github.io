What a scrolling reader sees (and misses)

  • It talks about the company, not the homeowner — 50+ years of history is a credential the reader is asked to admire, not a benefit they are offered. Nobody scrolls Facebook hoping to read a company biography.
  • No homeowner benefit anywhere — the ad never answers the only question that matters in the feed: “my pipe burst / my furnace died — what do I do right now?”
  • Your best facts are missing — your website offers 24/7 emergency service and runs more than 20 fully stocked trucks across Monroe County; for a homeowner in trouble, those two facts are the whole decision — and neither is in the ad.
  • No first step — no number to call, no emergency line to tap, no action named at all.
  • The same copy since 2020 — one history-led ad has been running since January 2020, outliving even its own “50+ years” line.

What the redesign does differently

  • The homeowner’s moment leads — the ad now speaks to the burst pipe and the dead furnace, the two situations that actually produce calls.
  • Your best facts, finally in the ad — 24/7 emergency service and 20+ fully stocked trucks were published on your website all along; the history paragraph was hiding them.
  • One clear first step — the phone number is in the creative and the button says “Call Now”; nothing stands between the problem and the call.
  • Financing acknowledged — one line tells the homeowner a big repair does not have to wait for payday.
  • History kept, but demoted — decades of local service become supporting proof under the benefit, not the headline.

What we changed — and why

Every change below is about what a reader can see in the ad itself: the words, the image, and the button. Nothing here assumes anything about your results — only about how the ad reads.

ElementBeforeAfter — and the reason
Headline / copy angleCredential-led: the company’s 50+ years of historyBenefit-led: 24/7 emergency help for the homeowner’s worst moment. People in the feed act on their problem, not on a supplier’s biography; the history still appears, as proof underneath.
OfferNo offer and no benefit statedNo price special is published on the website, so none was invented. Instead the ad leads with the published service offer — 24/7 emergency response — and keeps a marked slot ready for any current special (a seasonal tune-up or drain offer would drop straight in).

Call to action“Learn more”“Call Now”, with the number printed on the creative. For an emergency service, the call is the conversion — the button should say so.
CreativeGeneric truck/team imageryOriginal 24/7 clock design in a consistent company colour, headline and phone number on the image. The picture communicates “emergency help, day or night” before a word is read.
