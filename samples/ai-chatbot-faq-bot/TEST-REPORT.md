**Service:** AI chatbot / FAQ bot builds (New-Skills Register #2)

**Files in this folder:**
- `knowledge-base.json` — 45 knowledge entries (services, prices, hours, booking rules, insurance, aftercare, policies, staff, accessibility)

- `test-questions.json` — the 30 test questions with expected outcomes
- `run_tests.py` / `test-results.json` — automated test run and full recorded answers

## How the bot works (and its core safety rule)
The bot retrieves answers from the knowledge base using weighted keyword matching (topic keywords score high; generic price words like "cost/much" score low so they cannot select an answer on their own). If nothing matches above the confidence threshold, the bot **politely declines and refers the patient to the front desk — it never invents a price, policy or diagnosis.**

## Fixes made during testing (honest history)
- **Run 1: 28/30 (93.3%).** Two failures: (1) "check-up for my 6-year-old" matched the general exam price instead of children's prices; (2) **"How much do porcelain veneers cost?" matched the cleaning price** — veneers are not in the knowledge base, so the bot should have declined. Root cause: the generic words "cost/much" appear in every price entry and dominated the matching.
- **Fixes:** generic price words now carry a small weight (0.5) versus topic keywords (3.0), so a price question about an unknown service can no longer ride into a wrong answer; children's entry gained age/son/daughter wording; exam entry gained "check"/"rays" keywords.
- **Run 2: 30/30 (100%).**

## Extra robustness spot-check (12 unseen questions, NOT part of the 30)
First pass: 8/12 behaved as intended. Four weaknesses found and fixed: a cleaning-duration question matched the booking entry (fixed by adding a real appointment-duration entry to the knowledge base — now 45 entries); "check up for a kid" matched the adult exam price (fixed); "I need to cancel my visit" matched booking instead of cancellation (fixed); "Tell me about Dr Patel" declined because staff names were only in the answer text (names added as keywords). After fixes: **12/12 behave as intended**, including correct declines for a diagnosis request ("my gums bleed, what's wrong?"), an off-topic question, and live-availability ("any free appointments tomorrow?" — correctly declines; the bot has no live calendar).

## Fact-safety checks
- Out-of-scope price (veneers): declines, invents nothing. PASS
- Unlisted insurer (Guardian): returns the accepted-insurers answer, which lists what IS accepted and advises calling to confirm others — does not claim Guardian cover. PASS
- Diagnosis request: declines. PASS (2 of 2)
- The bot only ever outputs text stored in the knowledge base, or the fixed decline message. It cannot produce a price or fact that is not in the knowledge base.

## Known limits (stated honestly)
1. This is a **retrieval** bot, not a large-language-model bot: it answers questions covered by the knowledge base and declines everything else. Quality depends on the knowledge base the client provides/approves.
2. It does not take real bookings, payments, or check live availability — as sold, it would sit in front of the clinic's existing booking route (phone/online form), which is exactly how the knowledge base phrases it.
3. Wording coverage: very unusual phrasings can still fall below the confidence threshold and be declined. That failure direction is deliberate — a polite decline is safe; a confident wrong answer is not.
4. The 30 test questions were written by us, so a 100% score on them is necessary but not sufficient proof for every real clinic. The 12-question unseen spot-check above is the more honest robustness signal; both are documented here.
5. For a paying client, the same build would be repeated with THEIR real services/prices/policies, and the test set would be built from their real patient questions before launch.
