What a scrolling reader sees (and misses)

  • A trust claim, not a reason to call — “Trusted by 20,000+ Homeowners” could sit on any contractor’s ad, in any trade, in any city. It gives a stuck homeowner nothing specific to act on.
  • No offer in the ad at all — yet your own website runs a Broken Spring Special ($75 off two springs, $30 off one), a $49 lube & tune and more. None of it appears in the ad.
  • No emergency hook — garage doors fail at the worst moments, usually with a car trapped inside. The ad never mentions the same-day, 24/7 emergency repair your business actually provides.
  • No price anchor — with no number anywhere, clicking feels like walking into an unknown bill — so many readers simply scroll past.

What the redesign does differently

  • The emergency, answered — the redesign leads with the moment a homeowner actually needs help: a door that will not open, fixed the same day.
  • Your own spring special, in the ad — the offer already exists on your website; the redesign puts it where homeowners scroll.
  • A real price anchor — “$75 off” turns a vague worry into a concrete, bookable job.
  • A direct call step — “Call Now” fits a 24/7 emergency service far better than a soft browse button.

What we changed — and why

Every change below is about what a reader can see in the ad itself: the words, the image, and the button. Nothing here assumes anything about your results — only about how the ad reads.

ElementBeforeAfter — and the reason
Headline / copy angle“Trusted by 20,000+ Homeowners” — a generic trust claimA problem-led headline: “Broken Spring? Fixed Today.” A trust claim describes the business; a problem headline speaks to the reader’s emergency.
OfferNo offer anywhere in the ad, although the website runs a Broken Spring Special and other published dealsThe business’s own published spring special, placed in the ad. The offer already existed — it was just invisible where the audience is.
Emergency hookSame-day and 24/7 service never mentioned“Same-day emergency repair · Open 24/7” on the creative itself. Urgency is this trade’s strongest buying trigger, and the business genuinely offers it.
Main textGeneral reputation copyShort benefit-led text for a homeowner whose car is trapped, ending in one clear step. People in an emergency do not read brochures; they call.
Call to action“Learn more”“Call Now”. For a 24/7 emergency service, the phone call is the conversion — the button should say so.
