What a scrolling reader sees (and misses)

  • Urgency without substance — storm-damage framing is what every roofing ad in Georgia runs after a storm; on its own it gives a homeowner no reason to pick this company.
  • The strongest trust facts are missing — your website publishes a 5-year guarantee on all services, licensed and insured status, and a Better Business Bureau A+ rating; none of it is in the ad.
  • The offer is stated generically — a free inspection is a good first step, but the ad never says what the inspection includes, what happens after it, or what work it leads to.
  • No real first step — “Learn more” sends a worried homeowner to read, when the website already lets them book the free inspection directly.

What the redesign does differently

  • Trust leads, urgency supports — the storm is the context, but the guarantee and credentials are the argument.
  • The guarantee is on the creative — a 5-year guarantee on all services is the single fact most likely to stop a homeowner comparing roofers.
  • The free inspection becomes the call to action — the step your website already takes bookings for is now the button.
  • Credentials where they are checked — licensed and insured, BBB A+ rated — the three checks a homeowner runs before letting anyone on their roof.

What we changed — and why

Every change below is about what a reader can see in the ad itself: the words, the image, and the button. Nothing here assumes anything about your results — only about how the ad reads.

ElementBeforeAfter — and the reason
Lead messageGeneric urgency around storm damageA trust headline — free inspection backed by a 5-year guarantee. Every roofer claims urgency after a storm; very few put a guarantee in the headline, and that is what a homeowner is actually choosing on.
Trust evidenceNone in the adYour published facts: 5-year guarantee on all services, licensed and insured, BBB A+ rating. These already exist on your website — the ad was simply not carrying them.
OfferA generic free inspection / estimateThe same free inspection, stated completely: what it covers (inspection, repair, replacement, installation) and what stands behind the work. A complete offer reads as confidence; a bare one reads as a hook.
Call to action“Learn more”“Get Free Inspection”. For a homeowner with a leaking roof, booking the inspection is the conversion — the button should say so.
