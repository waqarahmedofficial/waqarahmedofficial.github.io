What a scrolling reader sees (and misses)

  • Credentials are not a message — AAHA accreditation is real proof, but on its own it tells a worried pet owner nothing about what happens next.
  • No offer or price in the ad — yet your own website publishes a $55 fee. The most concrete fact you have is missing from the ad.
  • Nothing written to the pet owner — the copy talks about the hospital, not about the animal the reader loves.
  • Running since October 2023 — two years on the same generic copy is a long time for an ad to go unrefreshed.

What the redesign does differently

  • Your published $55 fee, in the ad — it already exists on your website; the redesign puts it where pet owners scroll.
  • Credentials become the proof, not the headline — AAHA and family-owned now back up a benefit instead of replacing one.
  • Text written to the pet owner — about their animal, in their language.
  • Creative that says “vet” instantly — an original pet-led design with the headline on the image.

What we changed — and why

Every change below is about what a reader can see in the ad itself: the words, the image, and the button. Nothing here assumes anything about your results — only about how the ad reads.

ElementBeforeAfter — and the reason
HeadlineCredential-led (AAHA-accredited, family-owned) with no benefitA benefit headline for the pet owner, with the credentials as supporting proof. Proof persuades most when it answers a promise.
OfferNo offer or price in the ad, although the website publishes a $55 feeThe hospital’s own published $55 fee, placed in the ad. The fact already existed — it was invisible where the audience is.
Main textGeneral text about the hospitalText written to the reader about their pet, ending in one clear call step.
Call to action“Call Now” under copy that gives no reason to callThe same direct button, now earned by a benefit, a proof point, and a concrete fee.
CreativeGeneric clinic photoOriginal pet-led design in a consistent hospital colour, headline on the image itself.
