What a scrolling reader sees (and misses)

  • The headline greets instead of selling — a welcome message spends the most valuable line of the ad on politeness, not on a reason to stop scrolling.
  • “Premier” is a claim every medspa makes — it is unsupported and interchangeable, so it gives the reader nothing to believe or remember.
  • No treatment is named — the reader cannot tell whether this is laser hair removal, facials, or body contouring, although the website covers all of them.
  • The free consultation never appears — your website offers free consultations on all services; the ad, where the audience actually is, mentions no offer and no first step at all.

What the redesign does differently

  • A treatment leads, not a greeting — laser hair removal and resurfacing are named, so the right reader recognises her own goal in seconds.
  • The free consultation becomes the offer — the first step your site already publishes is now the ad’s headline and destination.
  • Verifiable facts replace “premier” — FDA-approved lasers, certified staff, all skin types: claims a reader can check beat claims she must take on faith.
  • The button completes the thought — “Book Consultation” tells her exactly what happens when she taps.

What we changed — and why

Every change below is about what a reader can see in the ad itself: the words, the image, and the button. Nothing here assumes anything about your results — only about how the ad reads.

Lead messageA welcome message with a “premier” claimA treatment-led headline built on your free consultation. A welcome is something a business says to people already inside; an ad has to win the stop first.

Call to action“Learn more”“Book Consultation”. For a medspa, the consultation is the conversion — the button should say so.
