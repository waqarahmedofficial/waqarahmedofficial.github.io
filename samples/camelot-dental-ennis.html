What a scrolling reader sees (and misses)

  • The offer has expired on its face — a spring promotion for April, shown in November, tells readers the practice is not watching its own advertising.
  • Everything rides on one seasonal hook — when the season passes, the ad has nothing left to say, although the practice treats families all year.
  • The year-round strengths never appear — the ad does not mention care for patients from 6 months old, Medicaid-CHIP and most insurances accepted, or Saturday and evening hours, all published on your website.
  • No clear first step — your website offers online appointment booking and welcomes walk-ins; the ad points to neither.

What the redesign does differently

  • Evergreen instead of expired — the message works in any month, because it is built on what the practice offers all year.
  • The family angle leads — care from 6 months old, with orthodontics under the same roof, is the reason parents choose this office.
  • The practical questions are answered — Medicaid-CHIP and most insurances, plus Saturday and evening hours, appear in the ad itself.
  • A dated offer is replaced by a dated-free step — book online or walk in; neither expires.

What we changed — and why

Every change below is about what a reader can see in the ad itself: the words, the image, and the button. Nothing here assumes anything about your results — only about how the ad reads.
