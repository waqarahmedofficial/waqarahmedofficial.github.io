# Social Posts — Do Dogs Understand Fairness? The Treat Test That Split the Pack

## LinkedIn
Give one dog a biscuit and the other nothing, and you may see a famous look: the stare, the pause, the paw that stays firmly on the ground.

We just published a video at EIDOS on the treat test behind that look. In 2009, researchers sat pairs of dogs side by side and rewarded only one of them for giving a paw. The unrewarded dogs stopped. Dogs tested alone kept going far longer, which points to the comparison, not just the empty hand. Yet when both dogs were paid and one simply got the better treat, the lesser-paid dog kept working.

The honest conclusion is narrow: inequity aversion, a dislike of being left out entirely, not a human sense of justice. Later studies were mixed, and the video says so plainly.

Do dogs understand fairness? We made a video about the treat test that split the pack.

Pairs of dogs sat side by side and gave a paw for food. When one dog was rewarded and the other received nothing at all, the unrewarded dogs began to stop, even though dogs tested alone kept going far longer. When both were rewarded and one simply received a better treat, the deal held.

That pattern has a name, inequity aversion, and it is narrower than fairness as we know it. The video walks through the original study, the comparison tests, and the mixed results that followed, honestly.
