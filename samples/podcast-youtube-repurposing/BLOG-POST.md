# Do Dogs Understand Fairness? What the Treat Test Actually Showed

Picture two dogs sitting side by side, each lifting a paw into a person's hand. One dog receives a treat. The other receives nothing. Same paw, same effort, different result. What the empty-handed dog does next is the subject of our latest EIDOS video, and of a study that has been debated since 2009.

Dogs live closely with us. They read our faces, voices and gestures, cooperate with us constantly, and learn small rules about who gets what, and when. That makes them a natural place to ask whether anything like fairness exists outside our own species. Capuchin monkeys had already surprised researchers in a token task, refusing cucumber while a partner received a grape. Would dogs do anything similar?

In the 2009 test, led by Friederike Range, pairs of familiar dogs gave a paw to an experimenter. First, both were paid in bread. Then one dog kept receiving its reward while the other received nothing at all. The unrewarded dogs gave the paw fewer times, needed to be asked more often, and finally refused to continue. Crucially, dogs tested alone without a partner kept giving the paw far longer, so the rewarded partner, not just the empty hand, ended the game.

A second test softened the injustice. Both dogs were rewarded, but one received sausage instead of bread. This time the bread dog kept working. Being given a smaller share was not enough to break the deal. Only being left with nothing, while a partner ate, was enough.

The video is careful about what this does not prove. The result is not a human idea of fairness, a moral rule about what is right and just. It is inequity aversion in a narrow sense: a dislike of an unequal outcome, strongest when the outcome is nothing at all. A 2018 review found dogs among the best-studied non-primates on this question, with well over a hundred dogs tested, but results were mixed, and some buzzer studies found no effect at all.

That honesty is the point. A dog is not writing a protest letter, but neither is it blindly obedient. Something social is happening in that room, and it is worth understanding on its own terms.
