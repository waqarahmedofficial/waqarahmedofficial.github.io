What a scrolling reader sees (and misses)

  • The part could be anyone’s — product-and-price creative is interchangeable with every other aero listing in the feed.
  • The fitment story never appears — the ad does not mention that your parts are engineered from OEM specifications or 3D-scanned at 1:1 accuracy, the facts published across your website.
  • The specialist reputation is unused — your standing in the Corvette and American muscle scene is the reason enthusiasts trust the brand; the ad never says it.
  • No guided first step — your homepage lets a buyer search by make, year, and model; the ad sends everyone to a generic shop page instead.

What the redesign does differently

  • Fitment leads — engineered from OEM specifications is the benefit every aero buyer is actually shopping for.
  • Proof replaces adjectives — 1:1 3D scanning and real-downforce engineering, both from your own website, do the persuading.
  • The specialist name works — Corvette and American muscle specialists is a trust claim competitors in the feed cannot copy.
  • The click lands on the buyer’s car — make / year / model first, so the reader shops parts that fit, not a catalogue.

What we changed — and why

Every change below is about what a reader can see in the ad itself: the words, the image, and the button. Nothing here assumes anything about your results — only about how the ad reads.

Lead messageGeneric makeover copy about exterior parts and accessoriesA fitment promise: aero engineered from OEM specifications for your exact car. In a feed of lookalike parts, the engineering is the difference.
