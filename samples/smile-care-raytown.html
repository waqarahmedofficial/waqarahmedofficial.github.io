What a scrolling reader sees (and misses)

  • Nothing distinguishes this practice — service labels and a smile slogan give a new patient no reason to choose this office over the next ad.
  • Your best offer is missing — the ad never mentions the $20 New Patient Special published on your website, the single strongest reason for a new patient to act.
  • No services are named — preventive care, crowns and bridges, and dentures all appear on your website; the ad names none of them.
  • The only step is a phone call — your website offers online appointment booking, but the ad asks readers to call instead of letting them book.

What the redesign does differently

  • Your own offer leads — the $20 New Patient Special gives a new patient a concrete, low-risk reason to choose this office today.
  • Services are named — preventive care, crowns and bridges, and dentures, so readers with a specific need recognise themselves.
  • The doctors are named — Dr. Dolson and Dr. Carr turn an anonymous office into a practice with people in it.
  • Booking replaces phoning — the online appointment step your website already offers becomes the ad’s call to action.

What we changed — and why

Every change below is about what a reader can see in the ad itself: the words, the image, and the button. Nothing here assumes anything about your results — only about how the ad reads.

ElementBeforeAfter — and the reason
Lead messageGeneric labels only: a general dental office, improving smilesYour published $20 New Patient Special leads. A specific, real offer is the strongest possible opening for a practice looking for new patients.
Service offerNo service namedPreventive care, crowns and bridges, and dentures named in the ad, so a reader with a broken tooth or a parent booking a check-up sees their own need answered.
First stepA phone call is the only stepYour own online appointment booking as the call to action. The step already existed on your website — it was just missing where the audience is.
Call to action“Call now”“Book Appointment”. Readers who will not phone a dentist from a feed will still tap to book — the button should offer the easier step.
