What a scrolling renter sees (and misses)

  • An award is not an offer — “voted best” builds trust, but a renter deciding this month needs a reason to enquire now, and the ad gives none.
  • Your real offer never appears — your website currently offers up to six weeks free rent for move-ins by November 15; the ad, where the audience actually is, says nothing about it.
  • Nothing concrete to compare — no floor plans, no bedroom counts, no lakefront or gated-community detail, so a renter cannot tell in seconds whether Bayfront fits them.
  • A passive button — “Learn More” asks for curiosity, not a leasing enquiry.

What the redesign does differently

  • Your offer leads — up to six weeks free rent is the strongest reason to enquire this month, and it was sitting on your website while the ad ran an award line.
  • The homes are specified — lakefront, one to three bedrooms, Dr. Phillips — so the right renter recognises the fit immediately.
  • The award supports instead of carrying — voted best in 2023, 2024 and 2025 now backs up an offer instead of replacing one.
  • A deadline and a number — move in by November 15, with your leasing team’s published phone number as the step.

Every change below is about what a reader can see in the ad itself: the words, the image, and the button. Nothing here assumes anything about your results — only about how the ad reads.
