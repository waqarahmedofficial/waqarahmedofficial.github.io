What a scrolling reader sees (and misses)

  • A teaser with no payoff — “something really special” asks for curiosity but never rewards it: no treatment named, no patient benefit stated, nothing to act on.
  • No offer in the ad at all — yet your own website runs the Espire Care Plan at $40/month for patients without insurance, covering exams, cleanings and X-rays. For an uninsured reader, that one fact decides whether they keep scrolling.
  • The biggest barrier goes unanswered — cost and insurance are the two reasons people delay dental care; the ad addresses neither, although most PPO plans are welcome and a no-insurance plan exists.
  • Nothing to distinguish the practice — the headline could sit on any dental ad in the country; no location, service or experience is named.

What the redesign does differently

  • A named audience and a named offer — uninsured patients in Denver hear, in one line, that care costs $40/month. No guessing, no teaser.
  • Your own Care Plan, in the ad — the plan already exists on your website; the redesign puts it where patients scroll.
  • The terms that remove fear — no waiting periods, no deductibles, no annual maximum: the three phrases that separate a membership plan from the insurance people are avoiding.
  • Both doors open — insured readers are answered too (“most PPO plans welcome”), so the ad speaks to the whole feed, not half of it.
  • A direct booking button — “Book Now” instead of “Learn more”.

What we changed — and why

Every change below is about what a reader can see in the ad itself: the words, the image, and the button. Nothing here assumes anything about your results — only about how the ad reads.
