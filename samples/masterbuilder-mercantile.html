What a scrolling reader sees (and misses)

  • A category label is not an ad — it names the shelf the product sits on, not the problem it solves or the job it is needed for.
  • No buyer is named — the contractors, mechanical professionals, and facility managers the website itself says it supplies are never addressed.
  • No use case — a backflow preventer protects a building’s drinking water and is a required fitting in commercial plumbing; the ad says none of that, so a reader who needs one may not recognise that this is for them.
  • No reason to choose this store — the website’s free ground shipping on orders over $125 and its 30-day refund never appear, so the ad competes on a category name alone.

What the redesign does differently

  • The buyer’s situation leads — a failed preventer holding up a job is the moment this product is bought; the copy now speaks to that moment.
  • Real brands are named — Watts and Zurn Wilkins backflow preventers, the genuine lines your store actually stocks, replace a bare category name.
  • Your published offer does the persuading — free ground shipping on orders over $125 and the 30-day refund move from the website’s fine print into the ad.
  • “Shop Now” now has something behind it — the same button, but a reader arrives at it already knowing the range, the brands, and the shipping offer.

What we changed — and why

Every change below is about what a reader can see in the ad itself: the words, the image, and the button. Nothing here assumes anything about your results — only about how the ad reads.
