What a scrolling reader sees (and misses)

  • “Trusted choice” says nothing — every competitor claims trust; a claim with no specifics is invisible.
  • The copy is truncated — the text stops mid-thought, so the one place meant to persuade simply trails off.
  • No offer stated — no estimate, no special, no first step a homeowner can take.
  • Nothing local or specific — no street-level reason for a Clearwater homeowner to call this number over any other.

What the redesign does differently

  • Specific beats generic — the copy now names the place, the problems, and the next step.
  • Complete, finished copy — every line written to land, nothing trailing off.
  • A clear offer position — ready for a real first-step offer (placeholder in this sample).
  • The call button gets a reason — “Call Now” stays, but now the words above it earn the call.

What we changed — and why

Every change below is about what a reader can see in the ad itself: the words, the image, and the button. Nothing here assumes anything about your results — only about how the ad reads.

ElementBeforeAfter — and the reason
Main textA generic “trusted choice” claim, truncated mid-thoughtComplete, specific, benefit-led copy. Trust is not claimed — it is demonstrated by saying something concrete.
HeadlineNo distinct headline; the generic claim carries the adA local, problem-led headline a Clearwater homeowner recognises as meant for them.
OfferNo offer statedA clear offer position (placeholder here — your real offer goes in). An ad without an offer gives no reason to act today.
Call to action“Call now” with no reason given to callThe same button, backed by copy that names the problem and the place — the two things that make a stranger dial.
