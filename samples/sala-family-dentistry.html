What a scrolling reader sees (and misses)

  • Generic urgency — “take charge of your oral health” could belong to any dentist anywhere; it names no treatment and no specific benefit.
  • The best fact is buried — same-day appointments are a genuine reason to call, but the copy treats them as decoration instead of the headline.
  • No offer or price — nothing concrete for a new patient to act on.
  • “Contact us” button — the most passive call to action there is; it asks for effort without naming the reward.

What the redesign does differently

  • Your own strongest fact becomes the headline — same-day availability, promoted from a throwaway line to the whole message.
  • Specific, treatment-led text — written for someone in pain today, not for everyone in general.
  • A clear offer position — ready for your real new-patient offer (placeholder in this sample).
  • A direct booking button — “Book Now” instead of the passive “Contact us”.

What we changed — and why

Every change below is about what a reader can see in the ad itself: the words, the image, and the button. Nothing here assumes anything about your results — only about how the ad reads.

ElementBeforeAfter — and the reason
HeadlineGeneric urgency (“take charge of your oral health”)The practice’s own same-day promise as the headline. Urgency works when it is specific: a person in pain needs a time, not a slogan.
Main textGeneral motivational copy, no treatment or benefit namedShort text aimed at one reader with one problem, ending in the booking step.
OfferNo offer or priceA clear offer position (placeholder here — your real offer goes in). Specific beats loud: an offer is a reason, urgency alone is not.
Call to action“Contact us”“Book Now”. The button should name the action the business actually wants — an appointment.
CreativeGeneric photo, no message on itOriginal smile-led design in a consistent practice colour, headline on the image itself.
