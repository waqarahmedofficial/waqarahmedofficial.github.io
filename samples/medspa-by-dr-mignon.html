What a scrolling reader sees (and misses)

  • The date undermines the ad — a stale headline makes an otherwise active practice look unattended before the reader reaches the service.
  • No named concern or treatment — the copy does not connect a visible concern such as frown lines to a service the practice actually provides.
  • No specific benefit — credentials and broad wellness language do not explain what Botox or Dysport is designed to do.
  • No clear first step — the website has a Schedule Appointment route, but the ad does not make that the obvious action.

What the redesign does differently

  • The stale date is gone — the replacement headline is evergreen and can remain accurate while the campaign runs.
  • One concern leads — forehead lines, frown lines, and crow’s feet give the right reader an immediate reason to stop.
  • The treatment mechanism is clear — Botox and Dysport are described as temporarily relaxing targeted facial muscles, as your website explains.
  • One appointment action — the ad now ends with the same Schedule Appointment route published on the site.
