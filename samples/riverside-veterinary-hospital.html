What a scrolling reader sees (and misses)

  • The headline is only a name — a name tells a pet owner nothing about why this hospital, for this pet, today.
  • The copy could belong to any clinic — “full-service” and “quality care” are what every veterinary hospital says; nothing here is specific to Riverside.
  • Your after-hours answer is missing — your website announces VetTriage, a 24/7 on-call triage service for the exact moment owners panic — at night, unsure whether it is an emergency. The ad never mentions it.
  • Directions are not a booking — the only step offered shows the reader where you are, not how to get their pet seen.

What the redesign does differently

  • A benefit replaces the name — the headline now promises care for every stage of a pet’s life, which is what the reader is actually choosing.
  • Your 24/7 triage service leads the proof — VetTriage answers the scariest moment in pet ownership; it is announced on your website but was invisible in your ad.
  • The step is a booking, not a map — “Call to book your pet’s visit” turns interest into an appointment instead of a set of directions.
  • Specific beats generic — every claim in the redesign traces to your own website: full-service care, every life stage, 24/7 triage guidance.

What we changed — and why

Every change below is about what a reader can see in the ad itself: the words, the image, and the button. Nothing here assumes anything about your results — only about how the ad reads.

ElementBeforeAfter — and the reason
Lead messageThe business name as the headline, with generic full-service copyA benefit headline for the pet owner, with the hospital named as the provider. Names identify; benefits persuade — and a name alone persuades no one who was not already looking for it.
Specific benefitNone stated — “quality care” onlyYour own published VetTriage service: 24/7 on-call guidance when an owner is unsure whether a pet needs urgent care. It is the one promise on your website no generic copy can match.
First step“Get directions” — a location, not a next step“Call to book your pet’s visit.” Directions serve someone already decided; a booking step is what turns a scrolling reader into a client.
Call to action“Get directions”“Call Now”, aimed at booking the visit. For a veterinary hospital, the appointment is the conversion — the button should say so.
