What a scrolling reader sees (and misses)

  • The headline is just the name — a patient scrolling past learns that a medical center exists, but not one reason to choose it or anything it can do for them today.
  • A list is not an offer — the copy lists services without saying what a patient gets: your website shows same-day appointments and on-site lab, X-ray, EKG, and ultrasound, none of which the ad turns into a benefit.
  • Forty years of trust goes unused — your clinic has served Anchorage since 1984; the ad never mentions it, so a new patient sees no difference from any other clinic.
  • No first step — your website has a clear “Book Now” step; the ad ends at “Learn more” instead.

What the redesign does differently

  • A patient benefit leads — being seen today, instead of the clinic’s name alone.
  • Real conveniences are named — same-day appointments and on-site lab, X-ray, EKG, and ultrasound, all from your own website.

What we changed — and why

Every change below is about what a reader can see in the ad itself: the words, the image, and the button. Nothing here assumes anything about your results — only about how the ad reads.

ElementBeforeAfter — and the reason
Lead messageThe business name and a “Medical Center” label as the headlineA patient benefit headline — care for the whole family, today. A name identifies the clinic; a benefit gives a patient a reason to stop and book.
Service offerA service list with no benefit attachedThe conveniences your website publishes — same-day appointments, on-site lab, X-ray, EKG, and ultrasound, family care, and FAA/DOT physicals — framed as what the patient gets, so the right patient recognises themselves in seconds.
