What a scrolling reader sees (and misses)

  • Experience is the whole proposition — an injured person still does not know what help is available or what it will cost to ask.
  • No offer — the website publishes a free case review, but the ad does not put that no-cost first step in front of the reader.
  • No practical benefit — 24/7 availability and no upfront costs answer two immediate worries, yet neither appears as the lead message.
  • “Contact us” is too vague — it does not say whether the person is requesting a call, a consultation, or a review of the case.

What the redesign does differently

  • The injured person leads — common accident types and San Diego County make the audience immediately clear.
  • The free case review is the offer — a concrete, no-cost action replaces a general claim about experience.
  • Cost anxiety is answered up front — no upfront costs and no legal fees unless the firm wins, exactly as published on the website.
  • Urgency has a route — 24/7 availability is paired with one named action instead of a vague contact instruction.

What we changed — and why

Every change below is about what a reader can see in the ad itself: the words, the image, and the button. Nothing here assumes anything about your results — only about how the ad reads.

Call to action“Contact us”“Get Your Free Case Review.” The destination and value of the click are now unmistakable.
