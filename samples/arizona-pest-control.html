What a scrolling reader sees (and misses)

  • A science lecture, not an ad — the copy explains termite biology and how the Sentricon system works. A homeowner worried about termites learns nothing about what happens next, or what waiting could cost.
  • It stops mid-sentence — the text trails off part-way through its own explanation, so the ad never finishes its pitch — it reads as copy pasted in and never checked.
  • No free-inspection offer — yet your own website offers free general pest and termite inspections. The one free step that turns worry into a booking is missing from the ad.
  • No homeowner benefit, no first step — nothing tells a Tucson homeowner why to call this company, or what to do next.
  • Running unchanged since 2022 — the same unfinished copy has been live for years, so it reads as set-and-forget.

What the redesign does differently

  • Your own free inspection, in the ad — the offer already exists on your website; the redesign puts it where Tucson homeowners scroll.
  • A headline that names the fear — hidden termite damage, answered immediately by the free inspection.
  • A trust fact that is already yours — locally owned in Tucson since 1947, published on your site, now working inside the ad.
  • Finished, checked copy — every sentence completes, and the whole ad ends in one clear booking step.

What we changed — and why

Every change below is about what a reader can see in the ad itself: the words, the image, and the button. Nothing here assumes anything about your results — only about how the ad reads.

ElementBeforeAfter — and the reason
Headline / copy angleA technical explanation of termite behaviour and the Sentricon systemA homeowner-led headline: “Termites Don’t Send a Warning.” Homeowners buy protection from damage, not a biology lesson.
The unfinished sentenceThe copy trails off mid-sentence (“…ends the food chain needed by the…”)Complete, checked copy that finishes its point and lands on a next step. An unfinished ad quietly tells readers the business is not watching its own marketing.
OfferNo offer anywhere in the ad, although the website offers free general pest and termite inspectionsThe business’s own published free inspection, placed in the ad. The offer already existed — it was just invisible where the audience is.
Trust proofNone visible in the ad“Locally owned in Tucson since 1947” — a fact already published on the business’s own website, moved into the ad where it can reassure.
Call to action“Learn more”“Book Now” for the free inspection. The inspection is the conversion for a pest control company — the button should say so.
