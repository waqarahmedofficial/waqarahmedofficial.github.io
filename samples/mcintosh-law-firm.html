What a scrolling reader sees (and misses)

  • The headline is just the firm name — a reader who does not already know the firm learns nothing from it, and “trusted partner” is a claim every law firm makes.
  • No specific case focus — the ad never names the situations that actually send people to an estate attorney: a will to write, a trust to set up, an estate in probate, a parent’s care to plan for.
  • No offer — there is no reason given to choose this firm over the next one in the feed, and no outcome the reader can picture.
  • No first step — your website invites visitors to schedule a consultation; the ad offers no equivalent step at all.

What the redesign does differently

  • A reader’s outcome leads — protecting assets and honoring wishes replaces the generic trusted-partner claim.
  • The cases are named — wills and trusts, probate, elder law, and business formation, all from your own website, so a reader with that exact need recognises themselves.
  • Your consultation becomes the call to action — the first step your site already publishes is now the ad’s destination.
  • A named local attorney — personalized attention from attorney Leslie McIntosh is a real differentiator against faceless online document services.

What we changed — and why

Every change below is about what a reader can see in the ad itself: the words, the image, and the button. Nothing here assumes anything about your results — only about how the ad reads.

ElementBeforeAfter — and the reason
HeadlineThe firm name plus a generic trusted-partner claimA benefit headline — protecting what the reader has built. A firm name means something only to people who already know it; a benefit gives everyone else a reason to stop scrolling.
Case focusEstate planning in general terms onlyYour published practice areas named — wills and trusts, probate, elder law, business formation. People search their situation, not a category; naming the situation is what makes the ad feel written for them.
First stepNo step offered in the adYour own published consultation request as the call to action. The step already existed on your website — it was just missing where the audience is.
Call to action“Learn more”“Contact Us”. For a law firm, the consultation is the conversion — the button should point at it, not at more reading.
