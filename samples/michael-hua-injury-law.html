What a scrolling reader sees (and misses)

  • The headline is a label, not a reason — “injury lawyer, Las Vegas” is word-for-word what dozens of firms say; nothing distinguishes this one.
  • Call-only is a high wall — an injured person scrolling at night, in pain or at work, often will not phone a stranger; with no alternative step, she simply scrolls on.
  • The free consultation never appears — your website offers a free, no-obligation consultation; the ad never mentions it, so the reader assumes contacting a lawyer costs money.
  • “No win, no fee” is missing — your published terms remove the cost risk entirely, and the ad — where the audience is — says nothing about them.

What the redesign does differently

  • The headline speaks to the injured reader — her situation and a free start, instead of the firm’s job title.
  • A form joins the phone — your website’s own contact form becomes a second, lower-friction route for people who will not call.
  • The terms do the persuading — free consultation, no obligation, no win no fee, nothing upfront: every element is already published on your website.
  • The case types are named — car and motorcycle accidents, slip and falls, and serious injuries, so the right reader recognises her case.

What we changed — and why

Every change below is about what a reader can see in the ad itself: the words, the image, and the button. Nothing here assumes anything about your results — only about how the ad reads.
