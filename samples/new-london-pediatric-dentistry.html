What a scrolling parent sees (and misses)

  • A welcome is not a headline — “welcome” greets a parent but gives them no reason to stop scrolling or to pick this practice.
  • A credential is the whole message — board certification is a strong trust point, but on its own it does not tell a parent what their child’s visit will actually be like.
  • No service named — the ad never mentions preventive care, children’s fillings and crowns, or early growth-and-development checks, although all of them are on your website.
  • No differentiator — your website says the office was built specifically with children in mind; the ad gives a parent no way to know that.

What the redesign does differently

  • A parent’s real question leads — “will my child be okay here?” is answered first: visits made fun and easy, in an office built for kids.
  • Services are named — preventive care, fillings and crowns, and growth-and-development checks, all from your own website.
  • The credential supports instead of carrying — board certification now sits underneath a reason to care, where it persuades hardest.
  • A real first step — your own published ways to book (phone or appointment form) replace a generic Contact Us.
