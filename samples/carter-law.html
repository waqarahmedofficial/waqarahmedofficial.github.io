What a scrolling reader sees (and misses)

  • A list is not an offer — naming practice areas tells an injured person what the firm does, but not why they should choose this firm or what happens if they do.
  • Your strongest term is missing — your website states “You don’t pay unless we win your case” with no upfront costs; the ad never mentions it, although it is the first question every injury client asks.
  • No benefit to the reader — nothing speaks to the reader’s actual worry: an insurer lowballing or denying the claim while medical bills arrive.
  • No first step — your website invites visitors to schedule a consultation; the ad offers no equivalent step at all.

What the redesign does differently

  • The reader’s problem leads — the insurer’s lowball is named, and the firm is positioned as the answer to it.
  • Your own terms become the offer — “you don’t pay unless we win” moves from a website sub-page into the ad, where it decides clicks.
  • The practice list is narrowed to the ad’s job — injury and wrongful death lead; estate planning can carry its own ad instead of diluting this one.
  • A real first step — “Schedule a Consultation Today” is the step your website already publishes; the ad now points at it.

What we changed — and why

Every change below is about what a reader can see in the ad itself: the words, the image, and the button. Nothing here assumes anything about your results — only about how the ad reads.

ElementBeforeAfter — and the reason
Lead messageA list of practice areas: personal injury, car accidents, wrongful death, trucking, estate planningA benefit headline for the injured reader — the insurer’s lowball named, the firm as the counterweight. A list informs; a problem stated in the reader’s own terms persuades.
OfferNo offer of any kindYour own published terms — you don’t pay unless we win, no upfront costs — stated in the ad. For injury work, the fee question is the decision; answering it in the ad removes the biggest reason to scroll past.
First stepNo step offered in the adYour own published consultation scheduling as the call to action. The step already existed on your website — it was just missing where the audience is.
Call to actionGeneric link behaviour, nothing case-specific“Schedule a Consultation Today”, matching your website. For a law firm, the consultation is the conversion — the button should say so.
