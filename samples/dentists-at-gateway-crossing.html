What a scrolling reader sees (and misses)

  • The copy is a staff roster — a list of names means nothing to a stranger who has never visited; names build trust only after the benefit is clear.
  • A generic goal statement — it could be pasted onto any dental practice in the country unchanged.
  • No offer or price — nothing gives a new patient a concrete reason to book a first visit today.
  • The button has nothing to sell — “Book Now” works only when the words above it have made booking feel worthwhile.

What the redesign does differently

  • Benefit headline first — the team’s names now support the message instead of replacing it.
  • Text written to the reader — what a new patient gains, in plain words, not an internal goal statement.
  • A clear offer position — one obvious reason to book a first visit now (placeholder in this sample, for your real offer).
  • Creative that reads as “dentist” instantly — original smile-led design with the headline on the image.

What we changed — and why

Every change below is about what a reader can see in the ad itself: the words, the image, and the button. Nothing here assumes anything about your results — only about how the ad reads.

ElementBeforeAfter — and the reason
Main textMostly a roster of staff names plus a general goal statementBenefit-led text written to a prospective patient. Names are proof, not a headline — they land after the reader knows why to care.
HeadlineNo distinct headline; the roster carries the adA benefit-first headline about family trust. A stranger decides in seconds, and “what is in it for me” has to come first.
OfferNo offer or price of any kindA clear offer position (placeholder here — your real offer goes in). An ad without an offer asks for attention and gives no reason to act.
Call to action“Book Now” under copy that gives no reason to bookThe same direct button, now earned by the copy above it — headline, benefit and offer all point at the one tap.
CreativeGeneric photo with no message on itOriginal smile-led design in a consistent practice colour, headline on the image itself.
