What a scrolling reader sees (and misses)

  • Seasonal copy, wrong season — a “just in time for summer” message still running in November makes the practice look unattended.
  • No offer in the ad at all — yet your own website leads with a $79 new-patient special. The one fact most likely to earn a click is missing from the ad.
  • A service checklist, not a message — a list of treatments tells a new patient nothing about why to choose you.
  • Generic family/beach photo — it could belong to any business; nothing in the image says “dentist”.
  • “Learn more” button — the weakest call to action for an appointment business.

What the redesign does differently

  • Your own $79 special, in the ad — the offer already exists on your website; the redesign simply puts it where patients scroll.
  • Season-proof headline — a benefit that reads correctly in November and in June.
  • Benefit-led text — written to the new patient, ending in one clear booking step.
  • Creative that says “dentist” — an original smile-led design with the headline on the image itself.
  • A direct booking button — “Book Now” instead of “Learn more”.

What we changed — and why

Every change below is about what a reader can see in the ad itself: the words, the image, and the button. Nothing here assumes anything about your results — only about how the ad reads.

ElementBeforeAfter — and the reason
Headline / copy angle“Just in time for summer” seasonal line, still live in NovemberSeason-proof benefit headline. Dated copy signals an unmanaged ad; a benefit reads true all year.
OfferNo offer anywhere in the ad, although the website leads with a $79 new-patient specialThe business’s own published $79 special, placed in the ad. The offer already existed — it was just invisible where the audience is.
Main textA checklist of servicesShort benefit-led text for a first-time patient. People do not book checklists; they book outcomes.

CreativeGeneric family/beach photoOriginal smile-led design in a consistent practice colour, headline on the image. The picture now communicates “dentist” before a word is read.
